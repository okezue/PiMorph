# Dataset hunt, round 3: confluent vascular endothelium with junction marker and expert instance masks (2026-09-19)

Status: complete (single-day search, 2026-09-19).

Target: a public dataset of a confluent vascular or lymphatic endothelial monolayer (or en face vessel preparation) imaged with a junction or membrane marker (VE-cadherin/CDH5, PECAM-1/CD31, ZO-1/TJP1, claudin-5, beta-catenin, JAM-A, occludin, membrane dye or GFP) that ships expert or manually curated per-cell instance masks or closed outlines. Also acceptable: hand-traced outlines for morphometry, and time lapses with curated outlines.

Not re-reported (covered in `docs/DATASET_HUNT_2026-09-18.md`): S-BIAD1540, S-BIAD1345, S-BIAD1169, S-BIAD1294, VE-Strat 13936923, mCellSeg, HAEC 4898011, Jacquemet 13643590 and 10611092, 13880404, 10617532, LIVECell, NeurIPS CellSeg, TissueNet, CTC, hCEC NCAM, Alizarine, FlyWing, NIH-NEI RPE, EpiCure, ReSCU-Nets.

Tiers: tier 1 = vascular/lymphatic endothelium + junction/membrane marker + confluent + expert instance truth; tier 2 = two of the four; tier 3 = anything else new.

## Downloaded and inspected

### `data/cover_zebrafish/` (Seeler et al. 2024, COVER: manually annotated Pecam1-EGFP endothelial cell contours, zebrafish dorsal aorta)
- Source: Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10549102, `cover.zip` (664.6 MB, HTTP 200 token-free, range requests honoured; licence BSD-3-Clause for the record; the paper is CC BY 4.0). Raw stacks: Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.11353017, `eng_wt_data.zip` 3,472 MB and `wt_angiogram_data.zip` 657 MB, Imaris `.ims` format (HDF5), CC BY 4.0. Paper: Seeler, Grdseloff, Rödel, Kloft, Abdelilah-Seyfried, Huisinga, PLOS Comput Biol 2024 (doi 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011924), "endothelial cell contours were manually annotated on Pecam1-EGFP-labeled cell junctions" (Zenodo description and README section 1.2: "manually annotated endothelial cell contours").
- I did not download the whole zip; I read its central directory by HTTP range requests (index saved as `cover_zip_index.tsv`, 809 entries) and extracted the README, LICENSE and all 30 contour CSVs (1.9 MB total) into `contours/`.
- Content: `input/eng_wt_data/embryo{NN}_{48,72}hpf_pecamEGFP_DA_outlines_Detailed.csv` for 13 embryos (7 wild type, 6 Endoglin-deficient; `genotypes.csv`) at two time points = 26 stacks, plus `input/wt_angiogram_data/Embryo{06,08}_72hpf_outlines_annotation_{1,2}.csv` (2 validation embryos, each traced twice by the annotator to measure annotation uncertainty) and matching `_angiogram.csv` lumen cross-sections. Each CSV is an Imaris "MeasurementPoint" export: columns `Position X/Y/Z` (um), `Surpass Object` = `cell N`, `ID` = point order; one ordered closed 3-D polyline per cell.
- Inspection with `.venv/bin/python`: 26 analysis stacks hold 6 to 17 traced cells each (296 cells; median 11 per stack); the 2 validation stacks hold 11 and 12 cells, twice (46 traces). 46 to 114 points per cell (median about 70). Example `embryo01_72hpf`: 11 cells spanning x 10.5 to 200.7 um along the vessel, y 4.3 to 32.4 um, z 5.6 to 48.6 um, i.e. contours wrap around a tube roughly 25 to 30 um in diameter, so a single 2-D projection does not give a planar instance map; the COVER package projects contours onto the fitted vessel surface (superellipse cross-section) and returns per-cell surface meshes (`output/cell_surfaces/*.vtp`, 646 files).
- Vascular endothelium: yes (dorsal aorta, in vivo). Junction marker: yes (Pecam1-EGFP knock-in, junctional). Confluent: yes (intact vessel wall, neighbouring cells share traced borders). Annotation: manual, per cell, closed 3-D contours, with a duplicate-annotation uncertainty set. Weaknesses: only about 300 cells in total, contours are 3-D polylines on a curved surface rather than 2-D masks, the raw images are `.ims` (need `imaris_ims_file_reader` or h5py), and each stack is a whole embryo trunk region where only the dorsal aorta cells are traced (partial field). Converting to a planar instance map requires unrolling the vessel surface (COVER gives the parametrisation), so PiMorph could evaluate vertex/adjacency metrics on the unrolled graph but not run its 2-D pipeline on raw slices.

## Verdict

1. **Still no tier-1 2-D dataset.** After paper-first searches, the BioImage Model Zoo, IDR (105 screens, 147 projects, key-value search), 98 BioStudies accessions, 13 Zenodo paired queries (95 records), figshare (18 queries), Dryad, OSF, Hugging Face, Mendeley, Kaggle, Papers with Code, 22 GitHub searches, the Cell Tracking Challenge, CellMigrationLab and the Allen Institute, there is no public confluent vascular or lymphatic endothelial monolayer imaged with VE-cadherin, PECAM-1, ZO-1, claudin-5, beta-catenin, JAM-A, occludin or a membrane label that ships expert per-cell 2-D instance masks or closed outlines. Every VE-cadherin/PECAM/ZO-1 endothelial deposit found this round (S-BIAD1920, OSF 3g6s2, figshare 28049660, Zenodo 8377287, plus the round-2 list) is images only or automatic output.

2. **One dataset meets all four criteria but in 3-D and at small scale: COVER (Seeler et al. 2024).** Vascular endothelium in vivo (zebrafish dorsal aorta), junctional Pecam1-EGFP, confluent vessel wall, manually traced closed contours for 342 cells across 30 stacks (26 analysis + 2 embryos traced twice), CC BY 4.0 images and BSD-3 contours, token-free. It is not a planar monolayer: contours are 3-D polylines on a 25 to 30 um tube, so PiMorph can use it only after unrolling the vessel surface (the COVER package provides the parametrisation and per-cell surface meshes), and the raw stacks are Imaris `.ims`. With about 11 cells per stack it is a validation set for adjacency and vertex geometry, not a training set. It is the first public expert-traced vascular endothelial cell outline set found in three rounds and should be recorded as such.

3. **Best tier-2 substitutes are unlabeled en face vascular endothelium**: S-BIAD1920 (96 whole en face mouse aortas, PECAM + ERG, CC BY 4.0) is the largest public confluent vascular endothelium with a junction marker and a nuclear channel; its published outlines are EPySeg output and would need expert correction before serving as truth. OSF 3g6s2 adds 15 GB of bEnd.3 ZO-1 stacks. Both are candidates for a PiMorph self-annotation effort rather than ready-made ground truth.

4. **Labs whose unpublished hand-traced data would fill the gap** (with the sentence that shows they traced cells by hand):
   - **Stroka lab (Kimberly M. Stroka, University of Maryland), JAnaP**: Gray et al. 2020 Fluids Barriers CNS 17:16 (PMC7014765), Methods: "Briefly, cells whose perimeter was completely visible within each image were traced via 'waypointing'. For ZO-1 and VE-cadherin quantification, waypointing was performed on the images of ZO-1 ... and the waypoints were projected onto the images of VE-cadherin". Confluent HBMEC and iPSC-BEC monolayers, ZO-1 + VE-cadherin + claudin-5, hand-traced perimeters for every analysed cell across many substrate and cAMP conditions (Gray et al. 2019 Ann Biomed Eng and 2020). Not deposited; code gated by a Google Form.
   - **Veschini lab (Lorenzo Veschini, King's College London), ECPT**: Chesnais et al. 2022 J Cell Sci 135:jcs259104: Weka junction model "trained on an annotated dataset of 70 CDH5 images randomly selected" from >20,000 CDH5-stained HUVEC, HAoEC and HPMEC monolayer images (>300,000 cells). The 70 annotated images and the full image set are available on request only; the annotations are Weka class traces, so the ask would be for the images plus permission to re-annotate.
   - **Searson and Prince labs (Johns Hopkins)**: Yang, Bogovic, Carass, Ye, Searson, Prince 2013 Proc SPIE 8669 (doi 10.1117/12.2006603, PMC3877311), HUVEC "immunostained for DAPI and VE-cadherin", Experiment: "The 'ground truth' segmentation was obtained by manual delineation of the boundaries of individual cells ... In all, about 900 cells are manually delineated" over six confluent monolayer images. Old (2013) but the only explicit per-cell manual VE-cadherin instance truth described in the literature; worth an email to Peter Searson.
   - Runner-up: the Weinberg / Bharath group at Imperial (Iftikhar et al. 2011, rabbit aorta Häutchen, 56 regions with >25,000 silver-stained cells and one manually traced region; contact p.weinberg@imperial.ac.uk), and the Huisinga / Abdelilah-Seyfried groups (COVER) who already annotate Pecam1 contours by hand and could extend to more cells.
